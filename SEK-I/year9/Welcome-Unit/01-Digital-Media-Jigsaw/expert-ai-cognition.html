🧠AI and Your Thinking Brain

Letting AI write your essay feels easy — but does your brain lose something when it stops doing the hard work? Your mission: find out what scientists actually measured, and be ready to explain it to your home group without reading off the page.

1Key facts

  • In 2025, researchers at MIT’s Media Lab published a study called “Your Brain on ChatGPT,” looking at how using AI for writing affects the brain.
  • 54 participants were split into three groups over four months: one used ChatGPT, one used a search engine, and one wrote essays using only their own brain.
  • Using EEG sensors (which measure brain waves), researchers found that ChatGPT users showed the weakest brain connectivity, and the “brain-only” group showed the strongest.
  • The more external help (AI) a person used, the more their brain activity “scaled down” — the researchers call this build-up of mental shortcuts “cognitive debt.”
  • ChatGPT users had more trouble remembering what they had just written and felt less personal ownership of their own essays.
  • Because AI answers sound fluent and confident, people often trust them even when they are shallow or wrong — researchers call this the “illusion of fluency.”
  • The study is small and was not yet peer-reviewed when published, so the researchers themselves say the results are preliminary, not final proof.
  • Later 2026 research, with 666 participants, also linked heavier AI use to lower self-rated critical thinking — but scientists stress this research area is still new.

2In their own words

“Brain connectivity systematically scaled down with the amount of external support.”

— MIT Media Lab researchers, “Your Brain on ChatGPT”

“It has not yet been peer-reviewed, thus all the conclusions are to be treated with caution and as preliminary.”

— MIT Media Lab researchers, on their own study’s limits
