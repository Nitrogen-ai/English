🌍AI’s Environmental Footprint

Every AI answer, image or video has to run on real machines, in real buildings, using real electricity and real water. Your mission: find out how big that footprint really is, and be ready to explain it to your home group without reading off the page.

1Key facts

  • Worldwide, data centres (many of them running AI) used about 448 terawatt-hours of electricity last year — more than all but around 10 countries in the world.
  • By 2030, electricity demand from AI-focused data centres is expected to more than quadruple, reaching roughly 945 terawatt-hours — about as much as the whole of Japan uses today.
  • Producing that electricity created about 208 million tonnes of CO₂ in one year — roughly as much as the entire country of Argentina.
  • Cooling AI data centres uses huge amounts of fresh water: an estimated 1.2 trillion gallons (4.5 trillion litres) in a single year.
  • On a small scale: one AI-generated image uses roughly 29 millilitres of water (about two tablespoons); a short AI-generated video can use over 4 litres.
  • By 2030, AI data centres’ water use could equal the basic drinking-water needs of all 1.3 billion people in Sub-Saharan Africa for a year.
  • AI infrastructure (buildings, servers) is projected to cover more than 14,500 km² of land by 2030 — roughly twice the size of a megacity like Jakarta.
  • Researchers warn that making AI more efficient does not automatically shrink its footprint — cheaper, faster AI often just gets used more, a “rebound effect.”

2In their own words

“What surprised us most is how often the choices that look greenest from a carbon perspective end up worse for water or for land.”

— Dr Miriam Aczel, lead researcher, United Nations University

“More efficient and affordable AI… mean more consumption of AI, making the overall footprint far bigger than what we save.”

— Professor Kaveh Madani, Director, United Nations University Institute for Water, Environment and Health
