🇦🇺Social Media Law in Australia

Australia was the first country in the world to ban under-16s from social media — by law. Your mission: find out how the ban actually works, whether it is working, and be ready to explain it to your home group without reading off the page.

1Key facts

  • Since 10 December 2025, Australia has had the world’s first national law banning under-16s from having accounts on major social media platforms.
  • Covered platforms include Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, Facebook, X, YouTube, Reddit, Threads, Kick and Twitch.
  • The law makes the platforms responsible, not the kids or their parents — companies must take “reasonable steps” to stop under-16 accounts, e.g. facial age-estimation, ID upload or linked bank details.
  • Companies that do not comply can be fined up to A$49.5 million (about US$33 million) — the government has also announced plans to double that maximum to A$99 million. There is no penalty for the young people themselves.
  • By mid-January 2026, more than 4.7 million accounts believed to belong to under-16s had been deactivated, removed or restricted.
  • A regulator study found that more than 8 in 10 young teens are still finding ways to use social media — for example with VPNs, a fake birth date, or less-regulated apps like Lemon8.
  • Some teens describe positive effects: 14-year-old Amy told the BBC she feels “free” without the pressure of keeping up a Snapchat presence.
  • Other countries, including the UK and several in the EU, are watching closely to see whether they should introduce a similar law.

2In their own words

“I feel free.”
