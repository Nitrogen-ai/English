🇧🇪Phone Rules in Belgium

Belgium just banned private mobile phones in schools — by law, in three different ways at once. Your mission: find out what exactly is banned, why, and be ready to explain it to your home group without reading off the page.

1Key facts

  • From the 2025–2026 school year, Belgium bans the private, recreational use of mobile phones in primary and secondary schools nationwide.
  • Belgium has three official language communities — French, Flemish and German-speaking — and each one wrote its own version of the rule.
  • In French-speaking Wallonia and Brussels, recreational phone use is banned in all schools, including pre-primary (kindergarten).
  • In the German-speaking community, phones and smartwatches are banned during both lessons and breaks.
  • In Flanders, pre-primary and primary schools already had a full ban; secondary schools now get a step-by-step plan: grades 1–2 have a full-day ban, grade 3 and up only ban phones during lessons.
  • Devices are still allowed for teaching purposes, health reasons (e.g. managing diabetes) or emergencies.
  • Each school decides its own punishment for breaking the rule — the law sets the ban, not the consequence.
  • Belgium joins a growing list of European countries with similar bans; UNESCO estimates that roughly one in four countries worldwide now restricts phones in schools.

2In their own words

“The disconnection measure is intended to improve pupils’ concentration as well as the overall school climate.”

— Zuhal Demir, Flemish Education Minister, quoted in The Brussels Times

“About one in four countries worldwide already restrict mobile phones in schools.”

— UNESCO estimate, cited in The Brussels Times
