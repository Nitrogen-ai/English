🇩🇰Tablet Retreat in Denmark

Denmark was one of the first countries to put tablets in every school bag — and now it is pulling back harder than almost anyone else. Your mission: find out what Denmark is changing and why, and be ready to explain it to your home group without reading off the page.

1Key facts

  • Denmark was one of the earliest countries to bring tablets and digital books into schools, starting around 2011.
  • Since February 2025, the Danish government has pushed to make schools “mobile-free” — personal smartphones banned during both lessons and breaks.
  • From the 2025/2026 school year, students lock their phones in boxes at the start of the day; from 2026/2027 the rule also covers after-school clubs.
  • The rule targets personal smartphones and smartwatches — computers and tablets used for teaching, under a teacher’s supervision, are still allowed, especially for older students.
  • The government invested DKK 540 million (about €72 million) in physical paper books to reduce reliance on screens, plus DKK 2.6 billion in outdoor play areas for real-world interaction.
  • Ministry surveys from 2024 found that more than a third of Year 6 and Year 8 students were regularly distracted by phones in class.
  • Danish teens aged 13–18 logging heavy daily screen time showed links to worse school results and more mental-health problems.
  • Teachers report better focus, more energy and stronger social contact once phones are locked away; Denmark is also considering a proposed ban on social media for under-15s.
