Back in Brighton — think, pair, share

  1. Think. What do you remember about Brighton from last year? Write down at least three sentences (e.g. about the beach, the pier, the shops or the parks).
  2. Pair. Compare your sentences with a partner.
  3. Share. Be ready to read out your best sentence to the class.
Sentence starters: There is … · There are … · The … is famous for … · You can … at the …

Brighton is a seaside town on the south coast of England. There is a long pebble beach and a famous pier with rides and games. The Lanes are small, colourful streets full of shops and cafés, and the Royal Pavilion is a beautiful old palace in the city centre. In summer, lots of tourists come to enjoy the sea, the sun and the lively atmosphere.

Two truths and a lie — get to know your class

  1. Write. Write three sentences about yourself — two must be true, one must be a lie.
  2. Read. Read your three sentences to a partner (or a small group).
  3. Guess. Your partner asks follow-up questions and guesses which sentence is the lie.
Example (not a real answer — just the pattern):
1. I have a pet dog. · 2. I can play the guitar. · 3. I have been to London.

Use these to find out more before you guess: Do you really have …? Since when can you …? What was it like?

Whoever guesses the most lies correctly after three rounds wins!

Take this with you

such a + noun · so + adjective · up ↑ · over ✓

  1. Use such a before a noun: „such a nice day“.
  2. Use so before an adjective alone: „so warm“.
  3. Up = towards the top; over = finished, or across something.
